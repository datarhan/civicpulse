# Process & architecture review — September 2026

> **A point-in-time record, measured on 2026-09-25. It is not maintained.**
> CLAUDE.md forbids counts in living docs because they rot; this is a dated

The questions asked were:
- are the processes optimised, and good enough for the goals?
- does the architecture fit?
- what do the best practitioners in this niche do?
- what did we miss or get wrong?

The decisions taken with the operator were:
- optimise for **Riba-roja depth and reach until the May 2027 municipal
  elections**; the national layer waits;
- stay at **€0/month**;
- **cookieless analytics** is acceptable.

Evidence:
- the repository;
- GitHub Actions history since the repo restart on 2026-09-08: 18 nightly runs,
  230 e2e runs and 166 deploy runs, plus the full 24 Sep nightly log;
- `npm audit`;
- the published snapshots;
- a browser pass over every page this branch changed, at 1280 and 375 px, light
  and dark.

## 1. Verdict

**The architecture fits the goals. Keep it.** It is a static SPA over JSON
committed to git, fed by scrapers and curator CLIs, with right of reply through
issue forms:
- it costs almost nothing and survives its operator;
- it makes git the audit trail;
- it has no server to attack or pay for.

For a solo, unfunded watchdog that has to still be standing in May 2027, that
is the right shape.

**The editorial and legal machinery is above the norm for the niche.** It
includes:
- two-file separation between machine output and curated files;
- bloc-level attribution;
- a deterministic verifier;
- figures frozen with four-outcome re-checks;
- tombstoned retractions;
- run manifests that separate "never attempted" from "unchanged";
- blind spots declared on the page.

**What was wrong sat around that core, not in it:**

1. **Real legal and security holes next to careful machinery.** The right of
   reply could publish text under a party's name without anyone checking who
   sent it, and the legal notice had no working contact channel. Fixed on this
   branch (§4).
2. **Almost nothing makes the site get read, and nothing measured whether it
   was.** Partly fixed (§4); most of the reach work is Phase 1 of the roadmap.
3. **Ops signals are noisy and some gates don't gate.** The laptop that runs
   half the jobs was also the only thing watching them.
4. **Complexity grows by accretion.** Each incident adds a guard and a
   paragraph.
5. **Effort goes where yield is low.** The automated claim machinery produces
   little, while election-year work waits.

## 2. What to keep — do not "fix" these

- **Data layer:** static SPA plus JSON in git; pure parsers pinned to real
  fixtures; `decideSnapshotWrite`, which never deletes published rows.
- **Editorial safeguards:**
  - two-file separation and bloc attribution;
  - the deterministic verifier, including the `opinativa` short-circuit;
  - the V1 evidence gates.
- **Checks on published claims:**
  - frozen `/eficiencia` figures with `coincide` / `movido` / `contradice` /
    `sin-indicador`;
  - tombstoned retractions and per-field correction logs;
  - the ClaimReview gate that refuses unadjudicated ratings.
- **Public self-disclosure:** `/lab-health` and the declared blind spots
  (README, "The blind spots").
- **The reader-review sweep.** On 2026-09-22, 7 of its 8 live flags were real
  defects. It is expensive, and it earns it.
- **Access-to-information requests tracked as data (`solicitudes`).** This is
  exactly what mySociety's WhatDoTheyKnow and Access Info do.

### P2 — yield, integrity, complexity

**24. Effort versus yield.**
- **Claims:** 7,564 extracted from transcripts; 99.8% end as `sin-datos`.
- **Pleno findings:**
  - 40 live: 39 informational, 34 signed `auto-curation-v1`;
  - 12 retracted;
  - none new since 2026-07-30.
- **Press findings:** 16 rows, but only 4 distinct stories.
- **Queja channel:** 1 published queja.
- **Promises:** 23 promises and not one terminal verdict, eight months before
  the election.

The valuable output is human-led: reportajes, access requests, `/eficiencia`
fichas, biographies. Recommendation: **freeze expansion of the automated claim
pipeline** (maintain it, don't grow it) and spend the capacity on Phase 2.

**25. The published contract and the code disagree in places.**
- `auto-curate-press.ts` and the promise auto-curator never call
  `decideAutomation`, which `/metodologia` says unattended publishing requires.
- README and the blog draft say machine inference is never the last step before
  publication. Auto-curation does publish informational findings, and says so
  on `/metodologia`.
- No claim records the model, prompt version or time that produced it.
- The verifier gold set is 64 rows, AI-labelled, with no `contradicho` example.

**26. Complexity budget.**
- 249 npm scripts and about 45 `check:*` guards.
- About 12 mechanisms that answer "is it fresh / did it run".
- A 33 KB CLAUDE.md dense with incident narratives.
- `OPERATIONS.md` had drifted from the code in about 24 places.

Each incident added a guard and a paragraph, and meta-guards now guard the
guards. The cost is attention, which is exactly what `health-monitor.ts` calls
the scarce resource.

## 5. Best practice in the niche, and what it implies here

| Practice | Who does it | Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alerts bring readers back.** Email or messaging alerts by topic are the core of TheyWorkForYou and of Civio's newsletters. | mySociety, Civio | Missing. The bot already has a channel and a queja digest; extend them to findings, reportajes and promise changes (Phase 1). |
| **"No surprises" before publishing about a person.** Offer the subject a reasoned chance to respond before an adverse or biographical piece goes out, and record it. In Spain, contrasting with the subject is evidence of the diligence that *información veraz* (art. 20.1.d CE) requires. | Investigative newsrooms; public-broadcaster editorial guidelines | Missing. Right of reply is post-publication only, and 5 of the 21 published biographies already carry post-publication corrections. |
| **A corrections page.** One public list of corrections and retractions, across all claim types. | IFCN signatories | Corrections exist per card in six record shapes; there is no index (Phase 1). |
| **Preserve what you cite.** Archive each cited URL at the moment of citation (Wayback Save Page Now) and keep a hash of what you read. | OCCRP, fact-checkers | `wayback.ts` exists but is used for press and biography sources only. Reportajes: 0 of 62 archived. Acta PDFs are discarded after text extraction. |
| **Open data people can reuse.** CSV next to JSON, a licence per dataset, schema.org `Dataset`, standards where they exist (OCDS for contracts, Popolo for people and votes), and dated releases with a DOI (GitHub → Zenodo is free). | Civio, OpenCorporates, Open Contracting | JSON only; no licence field; no ODbL attribution on OSM-derived files; no versions or DOIs. |
| **Measure reach, respectfully.** Cookieless, aggregate analytics that needs no consent banner. | Most small civic sites | Done on this branch (operator must enable). |
| **Monitor outside the failure domain.** Dead-man's switches for scheduled jobs, uptime checks from somewhere else. | Standard ops practice | Done (`ops-alarm.yml`); bot backups are still open. |
| **Write-if-changed snapshots in git scraping.** Separate "checked" from "changed", so history shows real changes. | Simon Willison's git-scraping pattern | About 70% of daily snapshot rewrites are timestamp-only (Phase 3). |
| **Continuity plan.** A second person with emergency access; domain auto-renew and registrar lock; 2FA everywhere. | Every durable one-person project | Unknown from the repo — operator checklist (§6). |

## 6. Operator actions

These can't be done from the repository.

1. **Restore your local drafts after merging.** When your Mac pulls the merge,
   git deletes the untracked drafts from disk because they were tracked before.
   Restore them as ignored files:
   `git restore --source=a4c4a58^ --worktree -- editorial/`
2. **Decide on the history purge** (runbook in [§8](#8-history-purge-runbook)).
   It is cheapest now, while the repository is young and has no forks.
3. **Create the `publicar` label.** Replies stay unpublished until you apply
   it, which is the point. Verify the reply's origin first, for example through
   the group's institutional email.
4. **Enable Web Analytics in the Vercel project.** Then open the site with
   DevTools and confirm no cookie is set before relying on the `/aviso-legal`
   wording.
5. **Repository settings.**
   - Enable Dependabot security alerts.
   - Consider branch protection requiring `e2e` on PRs, with "require branches
     to be up to date". Caveat: `e2e.yml` has `paths-ignore`, so a required
     check stays pending on data-only or docs-only PRs unless a no-op job
     reports it.
   - Making deploys wait for e2e in the workflow itself was deliberately **not**
     done. A `workflow_run` deploy builds `main`'s HEAD, so gating on the tested
     SHA can roll back data. Branch protection is the right tool.
6. **Accounts.** Turn on 2FA on GitHub, Vercel, Fly, the domain registrar and
   Telegram (BotFather). Enable registrar lock and auto-renew for the domain.
7. **Bot data.**
   - Raise the Fly volume's snapshot retention and **test a restore**.
   - Consider a periodic encrypted export of the full SQLite. Artifacts of a
     public repo are downloadable, so encrypt before upload.
8. **Continuity.** Give one trusted person emergency access (a password
   manager's emergency kit is enough) and a one-page runbook.

## 8. History purge runbook

Rewriting history changes every commit SHA. Any SHA cited in docs or correction
records then needs mapping; `git filter-repo` writes that map to
`.git/filter-repo/commit-map`.

1. **Pause every launchd agent** that pulls or pushes, and make sure nothing
   else is pushing.
2. **In a fresh clone,** run:
   `git filter-repo --invert-paths --path editorial/`
3. **Check nothing else was dropped:** `git log --stat -1` and a build.
4. **Force-push `main`,** and any branch you keep, then delete the stale
   remote branches.
5. **Ask GitHub Support to purge cached views and pull-request refs.** Old PR
   refs keep the objects reachable until GitHub garbage-collects them.
6. **Re-clone everywhere:** the Mac checkout, worktrees, CI caches. Then
   re-enable the agents.
7. **Record the date and scope** in the corrections log. Do not republish what
   was removed.
